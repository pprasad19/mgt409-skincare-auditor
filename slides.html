What's actually on your shelf

A skincare shelf auditor. Photograph your products, type them, or paste their links — and your face, if you want it read. It works out which clash, which duplicate each other, what order to use them in, and what the mistakes are costing you.

Purnima Prasad  ·  github.com/pprasad19/mgt409-skincare-auditor

The problem

People buy skincare one product at a time
and use it all at once.

Nothing in the purchase path looks at the combination.
A clash does not announce itself. It looks like irritation you blame on one product, replace, and do not fix.
Nobody is positioned to say so. Retailers are selling. Chatbots cite nothing. Ingredient databases know one product at a time.

What is new

The rules table decides.
The model explains.

Every finding, severity, routine position and dollar figure is computed by deterministic code from a reviewed table.

The model reads labels and writes prose. It cannot introduce a finding or change a severity.

Enforced, not requested

A narrative contradicting every finding, inventing a rule, rewriting the money and naming a product nobody owns changes nothing.

test_thesis.py · 915 checks

The agents

Four agents feed a deterministic engine. The engine decides every finding, severity and routine position; the Routine Auditor only writes the prose, and anything it invents is dropped before the page is built.

The finding

Almost every real defect was
confidently wrong, not visibly broken.

A shelf holding sunscreen, told it had none.
A 90-second wait captioned as two minutes.

None threw an error. All produced a clean, confident page — and several of my own tests excluded the case they were built to catch.
